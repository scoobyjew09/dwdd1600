Who Were The Signers

Famous Signers

The most famous signers of the Declaration of Independence, signed by 56 delegates on August 2, 1776, include

John Hancock
The President of the Continental Congress who provided the first and largest signature, making his name synonymous with the word "signature."
Thomas Jefferson
The principal author of the Declaration and a member of the Committee of Five who later became the third U.S. President.
John Adams
A primary advocate for independence in Congress and a member of the Committee of Five who served as the second U.S. President.
Benjamin Franklin
The oldest signer and a world-renowned scientist and diplomat who helped draft the document and negotiate French support for the Revolution.
Samuel Adams
A leader of the Sons of Liberty and a master of political organization who helped mobilize colonial resistance against British rule.
Roger Sherman
The only person to sign all four great state papers of the United States: the Continental Association, the Declaration, the Articles of Confederation, and the Constitution.
Button Gwinnett
A representative from Georgia whose signature is the rarest and most valuable among collectors due to his death in a duel shortly after signing.
Thomas Jefferson
Thomas Jefferson, a delegate from Virginia, was the principal author of the Declaration of Independence. He later served as the 3rd President of the United States and is remembered by his contribution to American ideals in liberty and democracy.
John Adams
Adams, representing Massachussets, was a vocal advocate for independence in the Continental Congress. He became the 2nd president of the United States and played a key role in drafting foundational documents and promoting the cause of freedom

While 56 delegates signed the Declaration of Independence, a handful of names stand out for their leadership, the "boldness" of their signature, or their later roles as presidents of the United States.
